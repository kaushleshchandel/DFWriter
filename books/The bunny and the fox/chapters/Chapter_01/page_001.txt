Once upon a time, in the hush of an old pine forest where the moss grew thick and the moonlight spilled silver over the needles, there lived a small gray bunny named Clover. She was quick, quiet, and fond of carrots she grew herself in a hidden patch behind a fallen log. Every dawn she checked her rows, whispering encouragement to the feathery green tops.

Not far away, under the roots of a lightning-split oak, lived a red fox named Rus. He was clever, hungry more often than not, and famous among the smaller creatures for his sharp grin. Everyone knew the stories: fox eats rabbit, that’s the way of things. So when Clover’s burrow path crossed Rus’s hunting trail one crisp autumn morning, the forest held its breath.

Clover froze, ears flat. Rus stopped mid-step, one paw raised, amber eyes wide. For a long moment neither moved.

Then Clover did something no rabbit had ever done in front of Rus: she sat up on her hind legs, pulled a perfect orange carrot from her satchel, and held it out.
Rus tilted his head. “Is that… a bribe?”

“It’s breakfast,” Clover said, voice steady even though her heart thumped like a drum. “You look like you could use one that didn’t involve chasing.”
Rus sniffed the air. The carrot smelled sweet, earthy, honest. He hadn’t eaten since yesterday’s half a mouse. Slowly he lowered his paw and took the carrot between careful teeth. Crunch. Sweet juice ran down his chin.
They stared at each other while he chewed.

“You know I could still eat you afterward,” he said, though his tail had stopped its eager swishing.

“You could,” Clover agreed. “But then who would grow you more of these?”
Rus licked his lips. “You’d grow carrots… for me?”
“I’d grow them for anyone who asked politely instead of pouncing,” she said. “Pouncing is noisy. Makes the lettuce bolt.”
A small laugh escaped the fox—short, surprised, almost a cough. “You’re a strange rabbit.”

“And you’re a fox who hasn’t tried to kill me in thirty whole seconds. That’s a new record, I think.”
Rus sat, curling his brush around his paws like a cat. “My name’s Rus.”
“Clover.”

Another silence, softer this time.
“Tomorrow?” Rus asked, nodding at the empty carrot top in his paw. “Same place?”
Clover’s ears lifted a cautious inch. “Bring something to trade. I like stories. Foxes know good ones.”

Rus grinned, but it wasn’t the hungry grin the forest feared. “I might know one or two.”
So it began.
